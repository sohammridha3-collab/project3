Machine Learning Pipeline Architecture

An end-to-end look at how messages travel from client submission to vectorization, classification, score calibration, and threat explanation.

The 6-Stage Detection Flow

Every message undergoes high-dimensional feature projection and hyperplane evaluation in less than 20 milliseconds.

01

Data Ingestion & Formatting

The incoming payload is sanitized and bounded to 20,000 characters. For email communications, the subject and body are formatted as: SUBJECT: <subject> BODY: <body>, preserving the contextual semantics learned during model training.

02

Dual Feature Extraction

A scikit-learn FeatureUnion extracts 120,000 sparse numerical features simultaneously:
• Word TF-IDF (1–2 n-grams, 80k features) for semantic phrasing.
• Character TF-IDF (3–5 n-grams, 40k features) for typos, obscured words, and URLs.

03

LinearSVC Hyperplane Separation

Selected as the winning classifier via stratified cross-validation over Naive Bayes and Logistic Regression. The model evaluates the dot product z = w · x + b to establish on which side of the decision boundary the message falls.

04

Platt Sigmoid Score Calibration

Because Support Vector Classifiers output geometric distance margins rather than direct probabilities, we apply a logistic sigmoid transformation: P(scam) = 1 / (1 + e^(-z)) mapping the signed margin into a transparent 0% to 100% likelihood gauge.

05

Supplementary Heuristics

To provide human-interpretable explanations during security reviews, regular-expression rules scan for classic social-engineering cues: urgency language, monetary prizes, password harvesting, and link shorteners.

06

Encrypted Persistence & Audit PDF

If the request originates from an authenticated user, the scan is linked to their profile in SQLite/PostgreSQL with server-side authorization. An executive-level PDF report can be downloaded instantly.

Dataset & Model Selection Details

Scientific rigor behind the training and selection of the final production pipeline.

Training Corpus: CEAS 2008

The pipeline was trained on the benchmark CEAS 2008 Email Dataset containing 39,154 verified records:

  • 21,842 spam / phishing emails (Label 1)
  • 17,312 legitimate ham emails (Label 0)
  • Stratified 80/20 train-test split with fixed random seed (42)
  • Exact duplicate content removed to prevent leakage across folds

Note: Spam is not identical to verified fraud; the model flags suspicious communication patterns that share structural markers with unwanted or deceptive solicitations.

Model Comparison Benchmark

Three candidate architectures were evaluated using cross-validation on the extracted TF-IDF feature space:

Model Mean CV Accuracy Mean CV F1
LinearSVC (Selected) 99.70% 99.73%
Logistic Regression 99.45% 99.51%
Multinomial Naive Bayes 97.53% 97.74%

SVMs excel in high-dimensional text feature spaces because they optimize the structural margin rather than minimizing training cross-entropy alone.

Limitations & Responsible AI Disclosures

Domain Shift Caution: The model was trained on email text. When applied to brief, informal SMS messages (such as casual conversations), the lack of standard email framing can occasionally result in false alarms.

Statistical Estimate, Not Legal Proof: AI text classification is an empirical signal to assist human judgment. Never authorize banking transfers, disclose OTPs, or reset passwords based solely on unverified communications.
